# Accuracy register

Every known way a page of this site can show the wrong text or the wrong label, and what is done about it. It is a living document: each review iteration adds failure modes, measures how often they happen and moves them towards *handled*. Architecture: [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md); target: [TARGET.md](TARGET.md).

**What a page promises.**
- (a) The text is the Seimas Chancellery's consolidation, unchanged.
- (b) Its label says correctly whether that text applies on the status day.
- (c) The page says when its data were last checked.

A failure is anything that breaks (a), (b) or (c) while the page looks confident. A page that says "Nepatikrinta" or "Gali būti pasenęs" when the text was in fact fine is a *false alarm*. That is a cost, not a failure.

**Status:**
- **handled**: a mechanism exists, and a test or measured check proves it;
- **partly**: a mechanism exists, but the evidence is incomplete;
- **open**: known, not yet mitigated;
- **watch**: rare or low impact, monitored.

**Likelihood (L) and impact (I):** H, M or L. High impact means a confident wrong answer ("Galioja šiandien" on a text that doesn't apply, or an in-force article with no page). "Iter" is the review iteration that found or last changed the row.

**Blind spots of the checks:**
- The same-version comparison with e-tar runs our parser on both texts, so a systematic parser loss is invisible to it (A11). The coverage check closes that gap.
- The e-tar comparison proves agreement on one day, and only for what e-tar's own consolidation contains. Conditional TAR notes (B9) and changes neither side has (B1–B3) are invisible to it.
